GUIDE 06 · ORDER BASICS

Market order vs limit order: the basic difference

An order tells a trading venue or broker what transaction a participant is requesting. Market and limit orders express different priorities, and neither guarantees every detail of an execution.

What is a market order?

A market order generally prioritises execution as soon as possible at the best available prices under the venue's rules. The final execution price is not guaranteed. In a fast-moving or less-liquid market, the order may execute across multiple prices or only partly fill.

What is a limit order?

A limit order specifies a maximum purchase price or a minimum sale price. It provides a price boundary, but execution is not guaranteed: the market may never reach the limit, or there may not be enough matching quantity. Venue rules and order conditions also matter.

Hypothetical example

Suppose a sample instrument shows an ask of ₹101. A buyer's market order may execute at the best available ask prices, which can change before completion. A buy limit at ₹100 sets a maximum price, but it may remain unfilled if sellers are not willing to transact at ₹100 or lower.

Execution and price are separate questions

It is useful to distinguish whether an order executes from the price at which it executes. A market order may execute but have price uncertainty. A limit order has a price condition but may not execute. Actual outcomes depend on liquidity, available orders, venue rules, order size and market conditions.

What is a partial fill?

A partial fill occurs when only part of an order's requested quantity is matched. The remaining quantity may stay open, be cancelled or be handled according to the order's time-in-force and platform rules. Do not assume an order always fills in one transaction.

What beginners should check

  • Is the screen showing bid, ask, last traded price or an illustrative value?
  • What quantity is available at the displayed prices?
  • What fees or other costs may apply?
  • What happens to an unfilled remainder?
  • Which venue and broker rules apply?

Important limitations

Order types can have additional conditions and names that vary by broker and market. This guide is a high-level explanation, not a recommendation to use one order type. A practice simulator may simplify execution and may not reproduce real order-book behaviour.

Key takeaway

Market orders generally prioritise execution while limit orders specify a price boundary. Both involve trade-offs, and actual execution depends on market conditions and platform rules.
