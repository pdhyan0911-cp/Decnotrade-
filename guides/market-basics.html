Market basics: what a price actually tells you

A market is a place or system where people and organisations exchange assets. Learning the basic vocabulary helps you describe what you see without assuming that a price tells the whole story.

Buyers, sellers and matching orders

A quoted price is connected to the willingness of buyers to pay and sellers to accept an amount. In an exchange, different participants submit orders under rules defined by that venue. An order may not execute immediately: there may be no matching order at the requested price, the market may move, or the order may only be partly filled. The details depend on the instrument and the type of order.

A last-traded price describes a past transaction. It does not guarantee that another participant can buy or sell the same quantity at that exact price now. Bid and ask quotes, available quantity and the speed of price changes can all matter.

Why prices change

Prices can change when expectations, available information, supply, demand or the willingness to take risk changes. News is only one influence. Participants may interpret the same information differently, and a move can happen even when there is no single obvious headline. It is usually impossible to know every reason behind a short-term movement from a chart alone.

Volume and liquidity are not the same thing

Volume counts how much traded during a defined period. Liquidity describes how readily an asset can be bought or sold without causing a large price change. They are related but not identical. A period with high volume can still have sharp price movement, while a quiet market may have little activity. A chart does not show every detail of the order book or execution conditions.

Understand the difference between live and sample data

Educational tools sometimes use fixed or generated values so people can learn the interface safely. A sample value is not an exchange quote. Even a simulated chart that updates every few seconds may only be animating example numbers. Always check a data source's label and timestamp before treating a value as current.

Example

If a learning screen shows a share at ₹100 and a hypothetical quantity of 5, the arithmetic value is ₹500 before costs. That calculation explains multiplication; it does not say that the share is currently ₹100, that it is worth buying, or that the price will rise.

Useful questions to ask

  • Is this a live quote, a delayed quote or an illustration?
  • What time period does the chart cover?
  • Does the displayed price refer to a trade, bid, ask or an example?
  • What information is missing from this screen?
  • Am I describing what happened, or guessing what happens next?

Key takeaway

Market vocabulary helps you ask clearer questions, but it does not remove uncertainty. A price is one piece of information—not a complete explanation of value, risk or future performance.
