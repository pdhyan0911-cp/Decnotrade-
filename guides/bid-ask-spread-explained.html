Bid-ask spread explained: bid, ask and the gap

The bid-ask spread is the difference between the highest displayed bid and the lowest displayed ask in a market. It is a basic market concept that helps explain quoted prices and some transaction costs.

What is the bid?

The bid is a price at which a buyer is currently willing to buy a stated quantity, subject to the quote's conditions. The highest displayed bid is often called the best bid.

What is the ask?

The ask, sometimes called the offer, is a price at which a seller is currently willing to sell a stated quantity. The lowest displayed ask is often called the best ask.

How is the spread calculated?

The simple quoted spread is the best ask minus the best bid. The units and quote conventions depend on the instrument.

Hypothetical example

If the best bid is ₹99.90 and the best ask is ₹100.10, the quoted spread is ₹0.20. These are made-up values. Quotes can change quickly, and the displayed quantity may be limited.

Why can the spread change?

Spreads can vary with trading activity, available orders, uncertainty, market opening periods, news and the instrument's liquidity. A quote is not a promise that a particular order will execute at that price or for any desired quantity.

Is the spread a cost?

The spread can contribute to the cost of transacting. For example, a participant buying at the ask and selling immediately at the bid would face the quoted difference before fees and any price changes, assuming those quotes remained available for the full quantity. Actual execution can differ.

Spread and volume are not identical

Volume measures completed trades over a period; spread compares current displayed bid and ask prices. A market can show high volume and still experience a wider spread at certain moments. Conversely, a narrow spread does not guarantee that a large quantity is available.

Common mistakes

  • Confusing the last traded price with the bid or ask.
  • Assuming the displayed quote is available for any order size.
  • Ignoring fees, slippage and changing prices.
  • Comparing spreads across instruments without checking units and quote conditions.

Key takeaway

The bid and ask describe current displayed buying and selling interest, while the spread is the gap between them. It is useful market context, but quotes can change and execution is not guaranteed.
