1. Introduction

For many years, people searched by entering words into a search engine, scanning a results page and opening relevant websites. Search experiences now also include AI-generated summaries, conversational answers and systems that retrieve web pages before composing a response.

That change creates a new discovery environment for publishers, businesses, developers and educators. A useful website still needs to be crawlable and relevant, but it should also explain its subject clearly, demonstrate credibility and make important information easy to verify.

Core principle: build genuinely useful pages for people first. Clear structure, technical accessibility and trustworthy evidence help both traditional search systems and AI-assisted discovery.

2. Evolution of search

Directory and catalog era

Early web discovery relied heavily on curated directories and manually organized categories.

Keyword and link-based search

Search engines increasingly crawled pages, interpreted words and links, and ranked results using many relevance and quality signals. Technical SEO, content relevance and reputation became important disciplines.

AI-assisted and conversational search

Modern products may combine a search index, retrieval systems, language models and citations. Examples include Google AI Overviews and AI search experiences from ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity. Features differ by product and change over time.

3. What is SEO?

Search Engine Optimization is the practice of improving a website so search engines can discover, crawl, understand and potentially rank its pages for relevant queries.

Technical SEO

  • Make important pages crawlable and indexable.
  • Use descriptive URLs, canonical tags and XML sitemaps where appropriate.
  • Improve mobile usability, loading performance and accessibility.
  • Use HTTPS, fix broken links and avoid accidental duplicate pages.

On-page SEO

  • Write specific page titles and useful meta descriptions.
  • Organize content with descriptive headings.
  • Use natural language that matches the topic and search intent.
  • Add helpful internal links and descriptive image alt text.

Off-page signals

Relevant editorial links, legitimate citations, digital PR and recognizable brand references can help people and systems assess reputation. Buying links or manufacturing mentions is not a sustainable authority strategy.

4. What is AEO?

Answer Engine Optimization is a practical label for making content easy for answer-focused systems to interpret and use. It can apply to featured snippets, voice assistants, search summaries and conversational answers.

Write direct, complete answers

Place a concise definition or answer near the relevant heading, then explain context, examples and limitations. Avoid padding a page with generic introductions before answering the actual question.

Use question-led structure

  • Use headings that reflect real user questions.
  • Explain processes in numbered steps when sequence matters.
  • Define unfamiliar terms and expand acronyms on first use.
  • Keep FAQs genuinely useful; do not add repetitive questions just to target keywords.
Example: “What is a sitemap?” Follow the heading with a short definition, then explain when a sitemap helps and how to submit one. A clear answer is useful; it does not guarantee a featured result or AI citation.

5. What is GEO?

Generative Engine Optimization describes efforts to make useful content more likely to be discovered, understood, referenced or cited by generative search experiences. The term is used across a developing field; there is no universal GEO ranking formula.

Useful practices

  • Publish original research, transparent methods, benchmarks or case studies when you have real evidence.
  • Attribute quotations and statistics to reliable sources.
  • Include dates and explain when information may become outdated.
  • Make important claims specific, independently checkable and easy to quote accurately.
  • Build topic depth with connected pages rather than publishing many shallow variations.

Do not invent surveys, customer outcomes, author credentials or first-hand experience. Unsupported claims can damage trust even if the page initially attracts attention.

6. What is LLMO?

Large Language Model Optimization is a broad, emerging term for helping language-model-based systems correctly understand a brand, organization, product or topic. Different practitioners use the term differently, and no single checklist guarantees that a model will learn or mention a website.

Make identity and facts consistent

  • Use the same accurate organization name, description and official website across owned profiles.
  • Explain what products and services do, who they are for and what limitations apply.
  • Keep author biographies, contact details and company information current.
  • Use descriptive page structure and relevant structured data.
  • Maintain authoritative documentation and public references where appropriate.

Do not assume that publishing a page immediately changes a model's training data. Some systems retrieve current web content; others may rely on model knowledge, external indexes or a combination.

7. Understanding E-E-A-T

Google's Search Quality Rater Guidelines describe Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness and Trustworthiness (E-E-A-T) as concepts used by human quality raters to assess content quality. E-E-A-T itself is not a single direct ranking factor or a score website owners can submit.

Experience

Show first-hand involvement only when it is real. Explain what was tested, under what conditions and what the result can and cannot prove.

Expertise

Provide accurate, appropriately detailed explanations. Identify qualified reviewers when the subject requires specialist knowledge.

Authoritativeness

Build a reputation through useful work, credible references, relevant recognition and independent editorial citations—not self-awarded badges.

Trustworthiness

Make ownership, authorship, contact routes, policies, sources, corrections and commercial relationships clear. Protect user data and avoid misleading claims.

9. How AI-powered search can work

Implementations vary, but a retrieval-assisted answer system may follow a process like this:

  1. A person asks a question.
  2. The system interprets the request and may search an index or other sources.
  3. It retrieves potentially relevant passages or documents.
  4. A language model synthesizes information and may compare sources.
  5. The interface returns an answer, sometimes with citations or links.

Retrieval, ranking, source selection and citation behavior differ across products. A citation is not proof that every statement in an answer is correct, and a page not being cited does not automatically mean it is low quality.

10. Website optimization framework

Homepage

  • Explain what the organization does and who it helps.
  • Link to the most important products, services and learning resources.
  • Provide accurate organization and contact information.

Article and guide pages

  • Give each page one clear purpose and a descriptive title.
  • Identify the author or responsible editorial team.
  • Support factual claims with primary or authoritative references.
  • Show publication or update dates when they are meaningful.
  • Link to related guides and explain limitations.

Product and service pages

  • Describe features, eligibility, pricing and limitations accurately.
  • Use real testimonials only with permission and appropriate context.
  • Keep product availability and support information current.

11. Schema markup and structured data

Structured data is machine-readable information that can help search engines interpret page entities and relationships. Use the Schema.org vocabulary and follow the specific search engine's eligibility and content policies.

  • Organization: identify a real organization and its official details.
  • Person: describe a real author or contributor accurately.
  • Article: identify an article's headline, author, dates and publisher.
  • BreadcrumbList: describe a page's place in the site hierarchy.
  • Product: describe a genuine product with accurate visible information.
Structured data does not guarantee rich results, higher rankings or AI citations. Markup must match visible page content. Do not add fake reviews, ratings, authors or FAQs. Google has limited FAQ rich-result display largely to well-known government and health sites; FAQ markup should not be treated as a general visibility shortcut.

12. Content strategy for modern search

Choose formats because they help readers complete a task, not because a format is rumored to be favored by AI.

  • How-to guides: clear prerequisites, steps, expected outcomes and troubleshooting.
  • Comparisons: disclose criteria, date, trade-offs and who each option suits.
  • Reference pages: stable definitions, examples and links to primary sources.
  • Case studies: real starting conditions, method, measured results and limitations.
  • Original research: transparent samples, methodology, data and reproducible analysis.

Update pages when facts change. Do not change dates merely to make old content appear fresh. Consolidate duplicate pages and remove content that no longer helps users.

13. Practical implementation roadmap

Week 1 — Establish the baseline

  • Verify the domain in Google Search Console and review indexing.
  • Check robots.txt, sitemap.xml, canonical URLs and mobile rendering.
  • Identify important pages, audiences and the questions those pages should answer.

Week 2 — Improve key pages

  • Rewrite unclear titles, headings and introductions.
  • Add accurate author or editorial information and source links.
  • Improve internal links, image descriptions and page performance.

Week 3 — Add structured information

  • Implement only schema types that match the visible page.
  • Validate JSON-LD and fix errors or misleading properties.
  • Check canonical and indexability behavior after changes.

Week 4 — Measure and iterate

  • Track search impressions, clicks, queries and useful conversions.
  • Test a small set of relevant questions in AI search products and record actual citations, dates and sources.
  • Improve pages based on observed reader needs and evidence—not anecdotal one-off outputs.

14. Tools and learning resources

  • Google Search Console — search performance and indexing reports.
  • Google Analytics — site usage and conversion measurement.
  • Google Search Central — official crawling, indexing and search guidance.
  • Schema.org — structured data vocabulary.
  • Rich Results Test — test supported Google rich-result markup.
  • Google structured data policies — implementation and eligibility guidance.
  • Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content — Google's content guidance.

Commercial SEO platforms such as Ahrefs and Semrush can support competitive research, but paid tools are optional. AI search products can be used for manual spot checks; their responses are variable and should not be treated as a stable ranking report.

15. The future of search

Search will continue to combine links, summaries, assistants and task-oriented interfaces. No single optimization acronym replaces the fundamentals: useful information, accessible pages, accurate facts, clear ownership and a good experience.

A durable approach is to combine sound SEO with answer-friendly explanations, evidence-rich content, consistent entity information and genuine trust. Measure what can be verified and avoid promises of guaranteed rankings, mentions or citations.

Frequently asked questions

Do SEO and GEO replace each other?

No. SEO remains important for discovery and indexing. GEO is an additional way to think about visibility in generative experiences, and many of the underlying quality practices overlap.

Can a website guarantee that ChatGPT or Gemini will cite it?

No. Source selection depends on the product, query, retrieval systems and many other factors. There is no reliable method that guarantees a citation.

Is E-E-A-T a direct Google ranking score?

No. It is a quality framework described in Google's Search Quality Rater Guidelines. It is not a single score that site owners can optimize directly.

Does adding schema automatically improve rankings?

No. Structured data can clarify page information and may enable eligible search features, but it does not guarantee rankings or rich results.

What should a small website do first?

Make important pages accessible and indexable, answer a real audience need, provide clear authorship and sources, improve internal links and monitor Search Console data.
